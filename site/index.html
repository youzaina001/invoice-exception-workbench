LedgerLens

LedgerLens lets an Operator investigate an incomplete supplier invoice and resolve its exception case inside the workbench. The hosted demonstration gives an invited Reviewer synthetic examples of that workflow. This page is a document. It does not sign a Reviewer in, and it does not call Neon, Clerk, or Render.

Who does what

The Operator investigates an incomplete supplier invoice and resolves its exception case inside the workbench.

A Reviewer is an invited person who enters the hosted demonstration and acts as the Operator inside their own Demo Workspace.

The Maintainer owns the hosted demonstration and may inspect every Demo Workspace.

A Demo Workspace is one Reviewer's private collection of synthetic invoice submissions, cases, check results, and audit evidence. It begins when that Reviewer signs in for the first time. Other Reviewers cannot read or change its contents.

A Demo Attempt is one execution of the invoice walkthrough within a Demo Workspace, with its own submissions, cases, check results, and audit evidence. It starts with no invoices.

Invitation only

Only an invited Reviewer may enter the operator application. The Maintainer creates each invitation in the Clerk dashboard. Clerk sends the email. The application does not send mail and does not offer an invite screen or public registration. Clerk stays on its free plan. Invitation-only access uses Clerk's invite-only mode. Clerk's paid allowlist is outside this release.

The Reviewer's first sign-in creates their Demo Workspace and its first Demo Attempt. The walkthrough publishes the two sample CSVs, and the Reviewer uploads those files in the hosted demonstration. Accepting real invoices is outside this release.

Read the walkthrough for resume, Start fresh, and the five-attempt cap. Download missing_amount.csv and corrected_amount.csv from this site.

Budget

The hosted demonstration has a strict recurring budget of €0. A payment method, a paid upgrade, and an overage are outside this release. A keep-alive ping or cron, a custom domain, Cloudflare R2, and Resend are outside this release.

Free-plan limits

These figures are the hosting decision for this release. They describe the intended free plans. They are not a claim that Neon, Clerk, Render, or Cloudflare Pages is already deployed.

The operator application is planned as one Render Free web service. After 15 minutes without inbound traffic the web service spins down, and the next visit takes about a minute while Render shows a loading page. Under that plan, the Pages site stays available during that wait. The instance disk is ephemeral, so submissions, cases, check results, and audit evidence stay in Neon. A keep-alive ping or cron is outside this release. Render cron jobs have a $1 monthly minimum. One always-awake service would consume essentially all 750 free instance hours, and a 31-day month has 744. Exhausting those hours suspends every free web service in the workspace until the next month. The Render workspace has no payment method, so a bandwidth or build-minute overage stops the free service instead of charging.

Neon for this demonstration is the free plan. Compute scales to zero after 5 minutes of inactivity and cannot be disabled, and wakes in a few hundred milliseconds. The project includes 1 GB of storage and 100 CU-hours per month. Crossing the compute or transfer allowance suspends compute until the next period. Crossing 1 GB stops writes that would grow storage. Neither case bills an overage.

Publication

The site/ directory is the static source Cloudflare Pages would publish when commits reach the existing master branch. The branch stays named master. Creating the Cloudflare Pages project and attaching it to master is a dashboard action and has not been done. Neon, Clerk, Render, and Cloudflare Pages are not deployed.
